Dexcom Clarity · macOS version

Get your Dexcom history in one click

Dexcom Clarity limits your export to three months of glucose history at a time. This bookmark snippet will automate the process for you, collecting all of your data back to your first record. The .csv file will be saved in your Downloads folder.

Your Data Stays Private

This runs entirely in your own browser, using the Clarity login you’re already signed in with. It sends nothing anywhere, saves nothing online, and changes nothing in your Clarity account. It asks Clarity for your own exports, stitches them into one file and hands it to you.

What to do

These steps are written for macOS.

  1. In a new tab, go to clarity.dexcom.com and log in the way you normally would, until you can see your own glucose data. Leave this page open — you come back to it in a moment.
  2. Show your bookmarks bar if it’s hidden — ⌘ ⇧ B in most browsers, or View › Always Show Bookmarks Bar.
  3. Drag the Get My Clarity History button below onto the Bookmarks bar.
    4. Get My Clarity History Drag this onto your bookmarks bar. Clicking it here does nothing — it only works once you’re on Clarity.
  4. Click Get My Clarity History on the bookmarks bar.
  5. Watch it work. It counts out each three-month stretch as it goes, and stops on its own when it reaches the beginning of your record.
  6. Your file lands in Downloads, named for today’s date and time zone.

Worth knowing

How long
A few seconds per three-month stretch, so under a minute for most people. Leave the tab alone while it runs.
Run it twice?
Harmless. You’ll get a second file, no different from the first. Nothing in your Clarity account changes either way.
If it stops early
A few months without a sensor won’t stop it — it steps over the gap and keeps going. It only stops once it finds a whole year with nothing in it. The dates in the panel show you what you got.
Nothing at all?
If nothing comes back at all, this account has no Clarity data in the past year. The panel tells you that instead of saving you an empty file.
The times in it
The times in your file are the times you saw on your own phone. Your timezone is added to the filename, so it stays clear later which clock they came from.
